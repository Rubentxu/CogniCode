# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We as members, contributors, and maintainers pledge to make participation in
this project a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body
size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender
identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status,
nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and
orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming,
diverse, inclusive, and healthy community.

## Our standards

Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive environment:

- demonstrating empathy and kindness toward other people;
- being respectful of differing opinions, viewpoints, and experiences;
- giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback, including technical
  disagreement;
- accepting responsibility, apologising to those affected by our mistakes, and
  learning from the experience;
- focusing on what is best for the community and the users of the tool, not on
  winning an argument;
- writing documentation and code that other people can actually read.

Examples of unacceptable behavior:

- the use of sexualised language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of
  any kind;
- trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks;
- public or private harassment;
- publishing others' private information, such as a physical or email address,
  without their explicit permission;
- sustained disruption of discussions, or dismissing a technical point by
  attacking the person making it;
- other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a
  professional setting.

## Technical disagreement is not harassment

This project values rigorous technical review, and that includes sharp
disagreement. "Your approach creates a second source of truth" is welcome
feedback. "You clearly have no idea what you're doing" is not. The distinction
is whether the critique is about the work or about the person.

Disagreeing with a maintainer's decision is fine, including publicly and
repeatedly. A closed work unit can be reopened only with new evidence, not
because someone disagrees with it.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all project spaces — the repository, issue
tracker, pull requests, discussions — and also when an individual is officially
representing the project in public spaces.

## Enforcement

Instances of abusive, harassing, or otherwise unacceptable behavior may be
reported to the maintainers.

**Use the same private channel as security reports**: GitHub Security Advisories
on this repository (`Security` → `Report a vulnerability`). That channel is
confidential and reaches the maintainers. Do not open a public issue to report
a conduct concern, and do not @-mention a person publicly to report their
behavior.

All reports will be reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly. The
maintainers will respect the privacy and security of the reporter of any
incident.

### Enforcement guidelines

Community impact guidelines, used to decide what response fits:

**1. Correction** — a private, written warning, with clarity about the nature of
the violation and why the behavior was inappropriate. A public apology may be
requested.

**2. Warning** — a warning with consequences for continued behavior, including
no interaction with the people involved for a specified period. Violating these
terms may lead to a temporary or permanent ban.

**3. Temporary ban** — a temporary ban from any sort of interaction or public
communication with the community.

**4. Permanent ban** — a permanent ban from any sort of public interaction
within the community.
